134	India runs on hundreds of disconnected government apps and portals.
135	A citizen who wants to register a birth, claim a pension, or check a land record doesn't deal with one government. They deal with dozens — each with its own app, its own login, its own language, and its own idea of how a form should work.
136	Services exist, but finding the real one — not a lookalike, not a middleman — takes confidence, data, and patience that the people who need help most often don't have. The maze itself is the barrier.
137	Saathi asks a simpler question: what if there were just one door?

210	One door, not hundreds.
211	India has built the rails of a digital state — identity, payments, documents and language technology at a scale no other country has attempted. What it doesn't yet have is a front door that feels made for the person standing in front of it.
212	Saathi reorganises government services around what happens in a citizen's life, speaks their language, and adds an assistant that can prepare an application, show its plan, ask for consent and hand back proof.
213	What was designed
214	government processes modelled on their real portals
215	languages — English and every one of the 22 in the Eighth Schedule
216	onboarding steps, including consent under the DPDP Act
217	assistant that never submits without your yes
218	An assistant that acts — and asks first.
219	Most government chatbots answer questions. The people we designed for don't need more answers; they need the thing done. So Saathi AI is designed as an agent: it plans, uses official services and produces an outcome.
220	Acting on someone's behalf with their identity and money raises the bar for trust. Every pattern below exists to keep the citizen in control.
221	Four patterns
222	Plan card
223	Before doing anything, Saathi lists its steps and the official service behind each one. The plan updates live as steps complete.
224	Tool steps
225	Each action — fetching a record from DigiLocker, checking eligibility — appears as a short, verifiable line with its source.
226	Consent gate
227	Nothing is submitted, paid or shared until the citizen approves a card that names the data, the recipient and how long access lasts.
228	Outcome
229	Every mission ends in something real: an application in Track, a paid receipt, a switched language — with a reference number.
230	When it can't help
231	Didn't understand
232	Offer three likely requests in plain words, and the option to speak to a person.
233	Not eligible
234	Say so plainly, explain which rule applies, and suggest what the person may qualify for instead.
235	Service unavailable
236	Keep the prepared application as a draft and say when to retry. Never lose what someone has entered.
237	Needs a human
238	Hand over to the nearest Common Service Centre or a helpline, with the context already filled in.
239	AI principles
240	Official sources only, always named
241	Asks before it acts on identity, money or data
242	Shows its reasoning for every eligibility claim
243	A person is always one tap away
244	Scripted and reviewable — no hidden prompts deciding outcomes
245	In this prototype the assistant is pre-scripted, not a live language model — so every answer it gives can be reviewed before it reaches a citizen.
246	Five tabs. The assistant in the middle.
